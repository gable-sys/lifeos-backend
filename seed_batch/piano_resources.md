# Gabe's Piano Resources & Curriculum

## Who I Am as a Player

- Blues, gospel, and soul player first — songwriter with good ears and improv instinct
- Been away from piano, getting back into it seriously
- Can play Gymnopédie No. 1 (Satie) hands together
- Learn naturally by ear but want to build real foundational reading and theory
- Bass clef reading functional but slow — needs to become automatic
- Left hand independence is the main technical gap
- Know some 7th chords but chord theory not yet fluent
- Also play guitar (blues, jazz, bluegrass, gypsy jazz)
- Classical training is the gym, not the destination — goal is to become a more dangerous version of what I already am

## Daily Practice Structure

### Full Session — 90 minutes

| Block | Focus | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Block 1 | Hanon + scales | 12 min |
| Block 2 | Sight reading | 13 min |
| Block 3 | Classical repertoire | 25 min |
| Block 4 | Blues & gospel | 20 min |
| Block 5 | Jazz theory | 20 min |

**Short session (60 min):** Drop Block 5, shorten Block 2 to 8 min. Everything else stays.

---

### Block 1 — Hanon + Scales (12 min)

- Hanon exercises, hands separate then together at ♩=60
- Slow and even — clean beats fast and sloppy every time
- Focus on fingers 4 and 5, they're the weakest
- C major scale two octaves, hands together
- Rotate through C, G, F major scales (the three blues keys)
- Blues scales daily:
  - C blues: C – Eb – F – F# – G – Bb – C
  - G blues: G – Bb – C – C# – D – F – G
  - F blues: F – Ab – Bb – B – C – Eb – F

**Hanon progression:**
- Week 1–2: No. 1 hands separate then together
- Week 3–4: Add No. 2 hands together
- Month 2: Exercises 1–5 rotating daily
- Month 3+: Build toward 1–10

---

### Block 2 — Sight Reading (13 min)

Rule: always new material, never go back to fix mistakes, keep the pulse going even when you miss notes.

**Rotation:**
1. Mikrokosmos Vol. 2–3 (primary — best for bass clef drilling)
2. Alfred: The Baroque Era
3. Clementi: Introduction to the Art of Playing
4. Grieg: Lyric Pieces
5. Anthology of Romantic Piano Music
6. World's Greatest Classical Music (free choice Saturdays)

**The one rule:** For the first 8 weeks, learn every piece from the score — not by ear. The ear is a massive asset but right now it's a crutch stopping reading from developing.

---

### Block 3 — Classical Repertoire (25 min)

One piece at a time. Hands separate first, then together, in small sections (4–8 bars). Two to four weeks per piece minimum. From the score only — no YouTube learning for repertoire.

---

### Block 4 — Blues & Gospel (20 min)

12-bar blues ritual: every single session, even rest days, run through the 12-bar at least once in C, F, or G. This is the foundation and it compounds.

**Rotation:**
- Junior Mance book — one example per day
- Ethel Caffie-Austin videos — one video, apply one thing immediately
- Free improv over blues changes
- Active listening: Otis Spann, Ray Charles, Professor Longhair (sit at the piano and find the notes)

---

### Block 5 — Jazz Theory (20 min)

Theory through music you already love — not abstract textbook grinding. Berkeley Harmony is a reference to consult when questions come up, not a front-to-back grind.

## Blues & Gospel Curriculum (Block 4)

### Month 1–2: Blues Foundation
- Junior Mance Ch. 1 — Basic Blues Structures, one example per day
- 12-bar blues in C, F, G with proper left hand shuffle patterns (not just block chords)
- Ethel Caffie-Austin — focus on left hand comping videos specifically

### Month 2–3: Gospel & Soul
- One gospel progression per week from Ethel Caffie-Austin
- Learn one Ray Charles song by ear from the record ("What'd I Say" or "Hit the Road Jack")
- Add turnarounds at bars 11–12 of the 12-bar (I–VI–II–V or blues turnaround)
- Start adding passing chords between main chords

### Month 4+: Jazz Blues Crossover
- Jazz blues chord substitutions connecting blues to jazz vocabulary
- Mark Levine shell voicings applied to blues progressions
- Dick Hyman as historical survey of how stride and swing evolved from blues
- Kurt Weill Centennial Anthology — unusual harmony, great for songwriting

---

## Jazz & Theory Curriculum (Block 5)

### Month 1–3: Berkeley Jazz Piano (Santisi)
- Start page 1, work front to back
- Chord voicings and jazz piano fundamentals
- Run parallel with Berkeley Jazz Harmony

### Month 1–6: Berkeley Book of Jazz Harmony (parallel track)
- Use as reference when questions come up — not a grind
- Pick songs you love, figure out the chords, name them, ask why they feel the way they do

### Month 4+: Mark Levine — The Jazz Piano Book
- Chapters 1–4 first: two-hand voicings, shell voicings
- Apply shell voicings directly to blues progressions in Block 4
- Rest of the book unfolds over 12 months

### Month 3+: Jazz Standards Note for Note
- One standard every 4–6 weeks
- Start simplest one — left hand voicings only first

### Month 6+: Dick Hyman — Century of Jazz Piano
- Historical survey: stride, swing, bebop chronologically
- Advanced material — let technique catch up first

## Week 1 — Day by Day

### Monday
- **Block 1:** Hanon No. 1 right hand alone ×2, left hand alone ×2. Hands together ×2 at ♩=60. C major scale. C blues scale.
- **Block 2:** Mikrokosmos Vol. 2 piece #1. Keep moving, 3–4 pieces.
- **Block 3:** Schubert Moment Musical No. 3 — open score, read it 5 min. Left hand bars 1–8 only, slowly.
- **Block 4:** Junior Mance Ch. 1 — read intro, work first example. 12-bar blues in C, twice through.
- **Block 5:** Berkeley Jazz Piano p. 1 — read intro, work exercises pp. 1–3. Berkeley Harmony p. 1 — just read.

### Tuesday
- **Block 1:** Hanon No. 1 hands together ×2. C major scale, C blues scale.
- **Block 2:** Mikrokosmos Vol. 2 — continue forward.
- **Block 3:** Schubert — left hand bars 1–16. Then right hand bars 1–8.
- **Block 4:** Junior Mance — next example. 12-bar in C with simple right hand melody added. Ethel Caffie-Austin one video.
- **Block 5:** Berkeley Jazz Piano — continue. Berkeley Harmony pp. 1–5.

### Wednesday
- **Block 1:** Hanon No. 1 ×3. Introduce No. 2 hands separate only. G major scale. G blues scale.
- **Block 2:** Alfred Baroque Era — switch books today.
- **Block 3:** Schubert — left hand full first section (~32 bars). Hands together bars 1–8.
- **Block 4:** Major triads C, F, G, D, A, E, Bb — root position both hands, say note names aloud. 12-bar blues in C.
- **Block 5:** Berkeley books — connect Block 4 triads to theory page.

### Thursday
- **Block 1:** Hanon No. 1 ×2, No. 2 hands together first time, slowly. C and G scales.
- **Block 2:** Back to Mikrokosmos Vol. 2.
- **Block 3:** Schubert — hands together full first section. Then look at Bach Notebook Minuet in G, listen to recording.
- **Block 4:** Minor triads C, F, G, D, A, E, Bb minor — root position. 12-bar blues in G.
- **Block 5:** Berkeley Jazz Piano Ch. 1. Berkeley Harmony — connect minor triads to theory.

### Friday
- **Block 1:** Hanon No. 1 and 2 hands together ×2 each. Add F major scale. F blues scale.
- **Block 2:** Clementi — first sonatina.
- **Block 3:** Schubert — full first section hands together. Middle section left hand. Bach Minuet right hand bars 1–8.
- **Block 4:** Dominant 7th chords C7, F7, G7 root position. 12-bar blues in F.
- **Block 5:** Berkeley books. Last 5 min: listen to Bill Evans, Oscar Peterson, or Monk.

### Saturday
- **Block 1:** Hanon No. 1, 2, start No. 3. All three scales C, G, F. All three blues scales.
- **Block 2:** Free choice — World's Greatest Classical Music or Romantic Anthology.
- **Block 3:** Schubert — full run-through beginning to end, hands together. Bach Minuet both hands bars 1–8.
- **Block 4:** 12-bar in C, G, F once each. Junior Mance — review week's examples. One Otis Spann video.
- **Block 5:** Berkeley books — review. Write down theory questions from the week.

### Sunday (Rest Day — 30–40 min only)
- **Block 1:** Hanon No. 1 and 2, once each. Maintenance only.
- **Block 3:** Schubert — one clean run through. Bach Minuet right hand from memory if possible.
- **Block 4:** 12-bar blues in C, G, F — once each. Then close the books.

## Key Principles

1. **From the score** — learn repertoire by reading, not by ear, for at least the first 8 weeks
2. **Never stop during sight reading** — keep the pulse, move forward
3. **Hands separate before hands together** — always
4. **Slow and even beats fast and sloppy** — every time
5. **12-bar blues every single session** — no exceptions, even rest days
6. **Theory in context** — always connect theory to music you love, never in the abstract
7. **Active listening counts as practice** — Friday listening ritual is curriculum
8. **Rest is part of learning** — Sunday is a short day on purpose
