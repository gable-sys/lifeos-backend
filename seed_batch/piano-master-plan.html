The one rule that changes everything

For the next 8 weeks, learn every piece from the score — not by ear. Your ear is a massive asset but right now it's also a crutch stopping your reading from developing. Classical training is your gym, not your destination. You're building toward being a more dangerous version of what you already are.

Blues & gospel — Block 4

Your home turf. Getting more organized and sophisticated about what you already do naturally.

Month 1–2 — Blues foundation
  • Junior Mance Ch. 1 — Basic Blues Structures. One example per day.
  • 12-bar blues in C, F, G with proper left hand shuffle patterns — not just block chords
  • Ethel Caffie-Austin videos: focus specifically on her left hand comping technique
Month 2–3 — Gospel & soul
  • One gospel progression per week from Ethel Caffie-Austin
  • Learn one Ray Charles song by ear from the record — "What'd I Say" or "Hit the Road Jack"
  • Add turnarounds at bars 11–12 of the 12-bar
  • Start adding passing chords between your main chords
Month 4+ — Jazz blues crossover
  • Jazz blues chord substitutions connecting blues to jazz vocabulary
  • Mark Levine shell voicings applied to your blues progressions
  • Dick Hyman as a historical survey of how stride and swing evolved from blues
Why the Junior Mance book matters for your songwriting

The Mance book covers blues in waltz time (3/4), 6/8, long meter, and ballad style — not just the standard shuffle. As a songwriter that's gold, because it shows you that the blues feeling is a harmonic and expressive language, not just a rhythm pattern. That directly feeds your composing instincts.

Daily 12-bar ritual — never skip this

Every single practice session ends with at least one 12-bar blues run-through. Even on rest days. Even when you're tired. This is your foundation and it compounds over time.

Jazz & theory — Block 5

Theory learned through music you love — not abstract textbook grinding.

Month 1–3 — Berkeley Jazz Piano (Santisi)
  • Start page 1, work front to back
  • Chord voicings and jazz piano fundamentals
  • Run parallel with Berkeley Jazz Harmony for the theory backbone
Month 1–6 — Berkeley Book of Jazz Harmony
  • Use as a reference when questions come up — not a front-to-back grind
  • Pick songs you already love, figure out the chords, name them properly, ask why
  • Theory in context is the only way it sticks for feel-first players
Month 4+ — Mark Levine: The Jazz Piano Book
  • Chapters 1–4: two-hand voicings, shell voicings — accessible now
  • Apply shell voicings directly to your blues progressions in Block 4
  • The rest of the book unfolds over the next 12 months
Month 3+ — Jazz Standards Note for Note
  • One standard every 4–6 weeks
  • Start with the simplest one — learn just the left hand voicings first
Month 6+ — Dick Hyman: Century of Jazz Piano
  • Historical survey — stride, swing, bebop chronologically
  • Advanced material — let your technique catch up first
  • Kurt Weill Centennial Anthology opens around the same time — unusual harmony great for songwriting

Video curriculum

Rotate through weekly in Block 4. Sit at the piano while you watch.

Weeks 1–4 — Blues foundation
  • Nahre Sol — search "Nahre Sol blues piano." Classically trained like you're building toward. Explains the theory behind what she plays in plain language.
  • Piano With Jonny — search "Piano With Jonny 12 bar blues." Clear instruction on left hand shuffle patterns and the blues scale.
  • Ethel Caffie-Austin — you're already watching her. Focus specifically on her left hand comping videos. That's your weakness and she addresses it directly.
Weeks 5–8 — Gospel
  • Ethel Caffie-Austin — continue. Gospel left hand comping is the focus.
  • YouTube search: "gospel piano left hand comping tutorial" — huge community of self-taught players who explain things in plain musical language, not theory jargon.
Month 3+ — Blues masters, active listening

Not instructional — sit at the piano and try to find the notes as you watch.

  • Otis Spann live footage — definitive Chicago blues pianist, Junior Mance's generation. Raw and clear to analyze.
  • Ray Charles — "What'd I Say" live. Watch his left hand specifically. That's a masterclass in independence.
  • Professor Longhair — "Tipitina." New Orleans blues. Completely different rhythmic feel. Opens your ears wide.
Month 4+ — Jazz crossover
  • Oscar Peterson and Adam Makowicz live videos — how jazz pianists absorb blues into their playing
  • FreeJazzLessons.com — Steve Nixon's free content on blues/gospel/jazz crossover piano
Friday listening ritual

Every Friday in Block 4: pick one record and just listen analytically. No playing — just ears. Ray Charles "Genius + Soul = Jazz," Junior Mance live on YouTube, or Professor Longhair "Tipitina." Active listening is part of the curriculum, not optional.
