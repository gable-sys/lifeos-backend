How This Works
The Approach

Blues First, Always

Blues is the DNA of jazz and much of bluegrass. The pentatonic and blues scale, bends, call-and-response — mastering these unlocks everything else. We return to blues constantly as a home base.

Theory = What You Already Play

After 10 years your ear knows things your brain can't name yet. Theory sessions are about labeling what you feel. We skip abstract and go straight to the fretboard every time.

One Week = One Full Rotation

Each week cycles Blues → Jazz → Bluegrass → Theory → Gypsy → Repertoire → Rest/Listen. After 4 weeks the styles start bleeding into each other — that's exactly what we want.

Shell Chords: The Jazz Guitarist's Secret Weapon

Shell chords use only the root, 3rd, and 7th — no 5th. They're how jazz guitarists comp without clashing with the bass. Learn: Cmaj7, Cm7, C7 shell shapes on strings 4-3-2. Then learn them on strings 5-4-3. Weeks 1–2 just get the shapes. Weeks 3–4 move them through a II-V-I (Dm7 → G7 → Cmaj7).

Your Theory Roadmap
Theory in Order

These topics build on each other. Do them in this order. Each one lives on the fretboard — never just on paper.

Week 1–2

Major Scale & Number System

W-W-H-W-W-W-H formula. The Nashville number system. Everything else is built from this.

Week 2–3

Intervals

Distance between notes. Major 3rd, minor 7th, tritone. Learn to hear AND find them on the neck.

Week 3–4

Chord Construction

Major (1-3-5), minor (1-♭3-5), dom7 (1-3-5-♭7), maj7, min7. Why they sound the way they do.

Week 4–5

CAGED System

The 5 chord shapes covering the whole fretboard. Connects all your scale positions into one map.

Week 5–6

The ii–V–I

The most important chord progression in jazz. Learn to hear it, play it in multiple keys, and solo over it.

Week 6–7

Modes (Dorian & Mixolydian)

Dorian = minor jazz & blues. Mixolydian = dominant 7th chords. These two cover 80% of your playing.

Week 7–8

Arpeggios

Playing chord tones as a melody. The secret to making solos sound "inside" the changes. Essential for gypsy jazz.

Week 9–10

Bebop Scales

Major scale + chromatic passing tone. Allows you to start and end lines on chord tones naturally on strong beats.

Week 11–12

Chord Substitutions

Tritone subs, secondary dominants, backdoor progressions. This is where jazz harmony gets deep and interesting.

Progress Checkpoints
Where You'll Be
End of Week 2

The Map Starts to Make Sense

You can name notes on strings 6 & 5. You know the blues scale in 2 positions. Shell chord shapes feel familiar. La pompe rhythm is clunky but happening. You understand what a major scale interval is.

End of Week 4

Theory Clicks with Your Playing

You can play a ii-V-I with shell chords and it sounds musical. Your blues improvisations have more vocabulary. Crosspicking starting to feel physical. You understand chord construction. La pompe is groovy.

End of Week 8

All Four Styles Accessible

You can comp jazz with drop-2 voicings. Blues solos use chord tones, not just pentatonic. Flatpicking at 100 bpm clean. Minor Swing all the way through rhythm. Modes feel connected to your playing, not academic.

End of Week 12

You Sound Like a Different Player

5+ standards you can play rhythm and solo over. A gypsy tune you can perform. Flatpicking 2+ fiddle tunes above 120 bpm. Your blues playing has jazz vocabulary woven in. Theory is something you use, not study.
